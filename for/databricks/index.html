Prepared for the Databricks interview team

The web has two audiences now. I build for both.

I’ve led web strategy, conversion and experimentation at Ancestry, AWS and Elastic for more than 15 years. At Elastic, I’m structuring elastic.co for the developers evaluating it and the AI assistants researching on their behalf. This page maps that work to the Director, Web Marketing role.

Why Databricks

Your posting describes the work I’ve been doing at Elastic: a high-traffic site for a technical audience, built so AI assistants represent the company accurately, with AI taking repetitive work off the team. I’d like to do that at Databricks’ scale, across more products, segments and markets.

My view of where the web is going: agents and answer engines will read most pages before people do. Sites that are structured, consistent and easy to parse will be described accurately and recommended. Sites that aren’t will be summarized by someone else’s content.

I built this site the same way: every page has a Markdown version served to AI tools that ask for one, plus an llms.txt summary. An agent-readiness audit scored it 98, up from 65.

How I’d start

  1. Listen first. Meet marketing leadership, the regional teams and the partners who depend on the web. Learn which properties, platforms and workflows exist today and where the team spends its time.
  2. Audit for people and agents. Ask AI assistants and answer engines what Databricks is and what each product does, and compare the answers to the positioning. Check information architecture, structured data and crawlability on the highest-traffic properties.
  3. Automate one workflow and measure it. Pick the most repetitive part of web production, QA or localization, put AI to work on it, and measure the time it saves before scaling to the next one.
  4. Agree on the testing and personalization roadmap. Set shared KPIs with demand generation and the regional teams, then rank tests by reach, impact, confidence and effort.
  5. Write the portfolio strategy. A plan for how Databricks’ web properties scale across segments, products, industries, partners and languages, including what each one needs to stay accurate in AI answers.

What I’d want to learn

  • How do AI assistants describe Databricks today, and where do they get it wrong?
  • Which web workflows take the most manual time right now?
  • How many web properties and platforms does the team run, and which should consolidate?
  • How does the web team work with regional marketing teams today?
