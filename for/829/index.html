Prepared for the 829 Studios interview team

Hands-on CRO, judged on revenue and lead quality, not clicks.

I’ve led conversion and experimentation programs at Ancestry, AWS and Elastic for more than 15 years, and I started by designing and analyzing tests myself. This page maps that experience to the Director, Conversion Rate Optimization role.

Why 829

This role puts me back in the work: research, test design, analysis and reporting, done directly. That’s where my career started and the part I enjoy most.

It also means building a practice from the beginning, which I’ve done before at AWS. And “logic with magic” is how I think about CRO: the data tells you where people get stuck, and good copy and design get them moving.

What the role needs, and where I’ve done it

  • Deliver CRO end to end, hands-on

    I started as a conversion analyst at Ancestry, designing tests, setting sample sizes and test lengths, and analyzing results myself in SQL and Adobe tools. At AWS, I built test cells in Adobe Experience Manager, set up campaigns in Adobe Target and analyzed results in Tableau and Adobe Analytics.

    Experiments

  • Validate data before trusting it

    I check sample ratios, tracking and funnel definitions before I read a result. Rechecking one Elastic test recently, I found the traffic split was 53/47 instead of 50/50, which is the kind of problem that has to be explained before anyone acts on the lift.

    Test readout skill

  • Use behavioral research to find conversion barriers

    Heatmaps and session recordings shape my hypotheses. At Ancestry, recordings showed fewer than 2% of visitors watched the DNA homepage video or read customer stories. Removing one changed nothing, but removing both cut DNA sales 2.4%, because visitors used whichever one was there.

    Experiments

  • Know when not to test

    At AWS, there was no default product page to use as a control, so we researched our way to an answer instead: analytics, a card sort, usability sessions and focus groups. I size every test before launch, and when traffic can’t support it, I recommend research or a direct fix.

    AWS product pages

  • Ecommerce and lead generation, measured downstream

    At Ancestry, tests were judged on subscriptions, DNA kit sales and lifetime revenue. A $79 price test sold 22.9% more kits but fell short of the 27% needed to break even, so we kept the price. At Elastic, a clearer trial button drew 55% fewer clicks, but 95% more of those visitors finished signing up, and data ingestion and paid conversions held even.

    Elastic Cloud trial flow

  • Write conversion copy and experiment briefs

    I write test copy and calls to action, and every program I’ve run uses a standard brief: hypothesis, success criteria, sample size and tracking, defined before launch. I turned that brief into a Claude skill that drafts it from a request.

    How I work

  • Build repeatable methods and a library of findings

    Every program I’ve built has test plan templates, a standard readout, and an archive of past results. The archive is how we got Trustpilot reviews to work on the third try instead of repeating the first two misses. I’ve published open-source skills for test readouts, sizing and archive searches.

    How I work · Skills on GitHub

  • Build a practice from scratch

    I founded the AWS website testing program: 50 tests in the first eight months with a 46% win rate, then test volume up 400% year over year. I grew demand through stakeholder ideation workshops and office hours, which is the internal version of pitching CRO.

    Programs I’ve built

  • Pitch, scope and price engagements

    I haven’t sold CRO to outside clients. I’ve made the business case for programs and headcount, scoped work by effort and impact, and managed creative agencies at AWS. Engagement pricing and agency economics would be the newest part of this role for me.

  • Use Claude in the work

    I build and publish Claude skills for CRO: test readouts, sizing and prioritization, archive searches, brief reviews and optimization briefs. They do the repetitive parts so more time goes to analysis and client conversations.

How I’d start

  1. Learn the accounts. Meet account leadership, Strategy, Analytics and Paid Media, and review current clients for audits, optimization projects or recurring programs.
  2. Define the offering. A focused CRO audit for smaller clients and a recurring optimization program for larger ones, each with a standard scope, deliverables and a pricing model that covers my time, tooling and specialist support.
  3. Set the standards. An experiment brief, a sizing check, a readout format and a findings library, so every engagement runs the same way and gets faster over time.
  4. Pitch and deliver the first engagements. Start with existing clients where the data and traffic already support testing, and report results with their limits stated plainly.
  5. Recommend the tooling. Compare testing and research tools against client traffic, needs and cost, including how license costs are recovered.
