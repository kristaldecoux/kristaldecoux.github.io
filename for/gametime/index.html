Prepared for the Gametime interview team

Web run like a business, measured in GMV.

I’ve led conversion, experimentation and web strategy at Ancestry, AWS and Elastic for more than 15 years, and I’ve always judged the work on revenue and signups, not clicks. This page maps that experience to the Director of Web Growth role.

Why Gametime

This role measures web the way I think it should be measured: one business number, with every channel and page as a lever. I’ve spent my career on the conversion and testing side of that equation, and I want to own the whole thing.

It’s also hands-on. Diagnosing funnel drop-off and fixing it with Product is the work I’m best at, and building SEO and content from a lean start is the kind of problem I like most.

What the role needs, and where I’ve done it

  • Judge web on a business outcome, not a channel KPI

    At Ancestry, tests were judged on subscriptions, DNA kit sales and lifetime revenue. A $79 price test sold 22.9% more kits but fell short of the 27% needed to break even, so we kept the price. At Elastic, a clearer trial button drew 55% fewer clicks, but 95% more of those visitors finished signing up, and data ingestion and paid conversions held even.

    Elastic Cloud trial flow · Experiments

  • Diagnose funnel drop-off and fix it with Product

    At Elastic, I worked with product and UX on three tests of the Cloud trial flow. The trial page redesign lifted trial signups 69% and trial starts 64%. I start with the funnel data and session recordings, form the hypothesis myself, and stay in the work through build, QA and readout.

    Elastic Cloud trial flow

  • Build an experimentation engine

    I founded the AWS website testing program: 50 tests in the first eight months with a 46% win rate, then test volume up 400% year over year. At Elastic, I relaunched the program with standard briefs, sizing before launch, a readout format and an archive anyone can search. Ideas are sorted and ranked before they reach the roadmap.

    How I work

  • Use full-funnel economics to make tradeoffs

    I size the value of a test before it runs: the extra conversions a lift would produce in a year and how many carry through to the steps that make money. I built my own calculator for that, plus test sizing and results analysis. Ancestry’s price test is the pattern: more units sold is not a win if margin falls.

    A/B test calculator

  • Lead paid search with enough fluency to push

    At Ancestry, I optimized landing pages for paid search, SEO, content, social and email, and lifted channel-level conversion 2–8% with experiences targeted by referrer and sub-channel. At AWS, I built and analyzed paid-media landing pages. I haven’t managed an SEM team or budget directly. I’d bring the conversion and measurement side, and learn account structure and bidding strategy from the team quickly.

  • Stand up SEO and content

    I set SEO and AI search strategy for Elastic.co, and my team includes an SEO content strategist. I built a Claude skill that reviews draft copy and returns keywords, metadata and page schema, so writers can do SEO work without waiting on us. I’m also leading information architecture work so people and AI search can find content.

    Search strategy

  • Build new capability from a lean start

    I started the AWS testing program from scratch and grew it through workshops for marketers, office hours and a shared-responsibility model, and managed creative agencies along the way. I’m comfortable deciding what to build in-house and what to buy from an agency or contractor.

    Programs I’ve built

  • Run dashboards and an operating cadence

    At AWS, my team delivered KPI dashboards on how web experiences drive conversion and leads, and I ran monthly and quarterly business reviews with leadership. At Elastic, I run a web roadmap that balances always-on requests with strategic work.

    How I work

  • Have a point of view on web and app

    I haven’t owned the web-to-app tradeoff directly. I’d approach it the way I approach any channel question: measure what each surface adds with holdouts where we can, compare lifetime value by surface, and put the tradeoffs in front of leadership with the numbers.

  • Use AI to move faster

    I build and publish Claude skills for test readouts, sizing and prioritization, brief reviews and SEO review. They take the repetitive work off the team so more time goes to analysis and decisions.

How I’d start

  1. Audit the web funnel and paid search. Map web GMV from landing page to purchase by channel, device and event type, and review paid search account structure, budget and performance with the team.
  2. Find where growth is being left on the table. Size each gap in GMV and contribution, not clicks, and rank the opportunities in a documented point of view for the VP of Growth.
  3. Build the testing roadmap with Product. Agree on the primary metric, guardrails and sizing rules, then start with the highest-traffic pages where a test can finish in weeks, not months.
  4. Scope SEO and content. Estimate the opportunity in event, performer and venue searches, then choose between an agency, contractors or a hire, and set the first 90-day plan.
  5. Set the cadence. A weekly web growth dashboard and a monthly review that shows the tradeoffs across channels, including when web should drive app installs.

What I’d want to learn

  • How is GMV credited between web and app today, and how are app installs from web counted?
  • How is the paid search team set up today: in-house, agency or both?
  • Where does the web funnel lose the most buyers today?
  • What testing and analytics tools does Product use, and how much engineering time can the web roadmap plan on?
