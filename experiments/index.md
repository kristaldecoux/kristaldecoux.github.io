# Experiments judged on revenue, *not* clicks.

A decade of website experiments, rooted in data and customer behavior, that moved revenue and qualified pipeline at Ancestry, AWS and Elastic.

**\$4.8M**annualized revenue from Ancestry tests I owned, from strategy to launch and analysis

**46%**win rate on 50 tests in AWS’s new program, first 8 months

**+400%**AWS test velocity, year over year

**+73%**Elastic Cloud trial signups from one homepage test

What the tests taught me

## Six principles

### Data-driven hypotheses

Combine quantitative and qualitative data, from analytics and heatmaps to session recordings, usability studies and focus groups, so ideas come from evidence, not preference.

### Meet visitors where they are

Match the message to what each visitor already knows. Someone new needs to understand what you offer before news or promotions mean anything.

### Not all friction is bad friction

Cut steps that add nothing. A step that adds context can mean fewer clicks but better-qualified signups and higher conversion.

### Guide the choice

Personalized, guided paths win. Too much choice before a purchase can stall the decision.

### The audience decides

“Contact sales” lost at Elastic and won at AWS. Test the path for each audience and industry.

### Measure to the money

Trial starts, form fills, billthrough and lifetime value decide the result, not clicks.

Case studies

## Control vs. variant

Each card shows the primary metric for control and the winning (or losing) variant, drawn to scale.

Elastic · Nov 2024Variant won

### Meet first-time visitors where they are

The hero changed with every product launch, but a first-time visitor doesn’t care about launches before knowing what Elastic does. Hypothesis: a static, brand-level message with a direct “Start free trial” CTA converts new visitors better.

**+73%**Cloud trial signups · p \< 0.002

**Lesson.** Orient new visitors before announcing anything. Click-to-signup also jumped from 14% to 41%.

Elastic · Dec 2022Variant lost

### Self-serve vs. “Contact us”

Swapped “Start free trial” for “Contact us” as the primary CTA on five industry pages.

**−44%**primary CTA clicks · p \< 0.01 · lower in 3 of 5 industries

**Lesson.** Here, visitors ready to evaluate didn’t want a sales conversation first. At AWS the opposite won, so I test the path for each audience.

Elastic · Feb–Apr 2023Variant won

### A trial page worth the extra step

A generic, committee-designed trial overview page sat between visitors and registration. I recommended rebuilding it around their questions: how the trial works, what you get, what to do once you’re in, where to find help and why it matters.

**+69%**trial signups from the page · trial starts +64% · p \< 0.001

**Lesson.** If you add a step, make it useful. A page that sets expectations brought in more signups and more trial starts.

Elastic · Jan–Feb 2022Mixed result

### “Get started” or “Start free trial”?

Changed the homepage hero button from “Get started” to “Start free trial,” with a note that the trial is free for 14 days and needs no credit card.

**+95%**signup completion among clickers · 55% fewer clicks, 30% fewer total signups

**Lesson.** A clear label qualifies the click. Fewer people clicked, but the ones who did knew what they wanted, and “Start free trial” became the default across elastic.co.

Ancestry · Free-trial flowMixed result

### Friction that adds context

After “Start free trial,” visitors answered an onboarding survey: intent (records, family tree or DNA), experience (beginner to expert) and commitment (hobby or passion). Their answers set the follow-up emails they got during the trial.

**↑ billthrough**fewer trial starts, but survey completers signed up and billed through at a higher rate

**Lesson.** An extra step can be a better experience when it gives the customer context and lets you personalize. Exact figures weren’t saved.

Elastic · Apr–May 2022Variant won

### Marketplace options on Downloads

Added partner marketplace logos and a primary trial CTA to the Downloads page.

**+167%**signup rate, plus a new path to AWS, Azure and GCP

**Lesson.** Give buyers the purchase route they already use, next to a clear primary CTA.

Ancestry · 2014–2015Variant won

### Skip the offer page

Someone who clicked an ad for US records still had to choose between US and World plans. We skipped the offer page and sent them to signup for the plan that matched the ad: first the US, then the UK and Australia. I re-tested it in the UK when conversion softened.

**+\$2.6M/yr**program impact · signups +14–16%, LTR +12–13%

**Lesson.** It worked because the ad already told us what the visitor wanted. Re-check a win when the business changes.

Ancestry · Sept 2014Variant lost

### A product-choice page before signup

Asked paid search visitors to choose between two products before signup.

**−37%**conversion · default kept

**Lesson.** A menu before purchase adds friction. Guide people to the right choice instead.

Ancestry · Nov 2015–Jan 2016Variant won

### Carry the search into checkout

Paid search visitors saw the record they’d searched for on every step of signup. I was the testing specialist; ~200K visitors per cell.

**+6.6%**signups (significant) · LTR per visitor +2.9%

**Lesson.** Keeping the visitor’s intent visible to payment is one of the most dependable lifts in paid acquisition.

Ancestry · Sept–Oct 2015Variant won

### Guided choice beats a menu

Registered visitors and win-backs saw only “Start free trial” or “Subscribe.” A guided-choice banner added more ways to start: a family tree or AncestryDNA.

**+9%**signups · LTR per visitor +8.9%

**Lesson.** Give people more than one way to start, and guide them to the one that fits.

Ancestry · Mar–Sept 2014Variant won

### Trustpilot reviews, third time right

Versions 1 and 2 put reviews in the signup and payment flow and lost. Version 3 tested landing page, payment page, both or neither.

**+4.8%**signup rate · billthrough 47.1% → 50.4%

**Lesson.** Social proof works only in the right place, at the right time and in the right format. It took three tries.

Ancestry · May 2016Variant won

### Quiet proof, big lift

A five-cell test of social follow icons, with or without follower counts, on a high-traffic SEO entry page.

**+11.6%**signups · LTR per visitor +22% · rolled out to 100%

**Lesson.** Proof that doesn’t compete with the task wins. Showing counts did worse than icons alone.

Elastic · Apr 2022Variant lost

### An analyst badge in the hero

Promoted a new Forrester Wave rating in the homepage hero.

**−97%**hero CTA click-through · signups −45%

**Lesson.** Analyst proof belongs below the hero, near evaluation. Never let it replace the CTA.

Ancestry · StrategyFramework

### Six kinds of social proof, put to work

I brought a framework back from a conversion optimization conference talk (Sum it, Score it, Say it, Sign it, Show it, Shine it), audited Ancestry’s pages starting with the AncestryDNA homepage, scored each proof element and built a testing strategy for each type.

**+10%**conversion on high-volume entry pages · ~\$20K a month

**Lesson.** Treat social proof as a system you design and test, not a badge you add.

Ancestry · Oct 2015–May 2016Variant won

### One redesign, three markets

Rebuilt the AncestryDNA homepage to explain the ethnicity estimate, the size of the database and how DNA and Ancestry work together, then localized it for the UK and Australia.

**+16.1%**UK DNA kit sales · US +3.4% (p = 0.04) · Australia +1.6%

**Lesson.** A proven design can travel when it’s localized, but expect a different lift in every market.

Ancestry · Aug–Oct 2015Mixed result

### A price cut that sold more and still lost

Tested a \$20 lower price for an AncestryDNA kit. To break even, sales needed to rise about 27%.

**+22.9%**DNA kit sales · short of the ~27% needed to break even

**Lesson.** Judge a price test on revenue, not conversion. The price stayed, which also kept room for future promotions.

Ancestry · Feb–Apr 2016Variant lost

### Low engagement isn’t low value

Fewer than 2% of visitors watched the DNA homepage video or read member stories, so we tested removing each one, then both.

**−2.4%**DNA sales with both removed · removing one was flat

**Lesson.** When one element was gone, visitors used the other more. Remove all of the proof and fewer people buy.

Ancestry · Feb–Apr 2016Mixed result

### Same test, three partners, three results

Showed the referring partner’s logo in the signup flow for visitors arriving from three partner sites.

**+7.1%**signups for one partner · LTR per visitor +10.9% · flat for a second, −6.7% for a third

**Lesson.** Partner traffic isn’t interchangeable. Roll out a win partner by partner, not to all of them at once.

Ancestry · Apr–May 2016Variant won

### A winning headline we couldn’t ship

Tested a shorter headline on a high-traffic page that ranks in search.

**+5.6%**signups · LTR per visitor +8.4%

**Lesson.** The old headline held the keywords that brought the traffic. We built a version of the page for paid traffic so we could keep testing without risking search rank.

Elastic · May–Sep 2024Variant won

### Carry the context into signup

Trial buttons on six product and solution pages linked to a registration flow matched to that product, instead of a generic one.

**+83%**signup completion · trial starts 67% → 80% · p \< 0.001

**Lesson.** People finish what they start when the next step matches where they came from.

Elastic · Dec 2024–Jan 2025Variant won

### Navigation that matches how the company explains itself

Tested a new top navigation, organized around Elastic’s platform and solutions, against the old structure.

**+9.1%**navigation engagement · p \< 0.001 · search use +37% · trial clicks steady

**Lesson.** Information architecture is testable. Measure how people move through the site, and check that the path to a trial holds.

AWS · HomepageVariant won

### Orient first-time visitors

A six-slide carousel of launches, events and Free Tier greeted every visitor. First-time visitors instead saw a static, brand-level hero.

**+5%**trial button clicks · ~75% of clickers created an account

**Lesson.** New visitors need orientation before announcements. I brought the approach to Elastic, where it lifted trial signups 73%.

AWS · HomepageMixed result

### “I’m a Builder” or “I’m a Buyer”?

A persona selector changed below-the-fold content by role. Visitors engaged, but clicks split evenly. A focus group at the next AWS Summit found developers clicked both, curious and afraid to miss something.

**50 / 50**clicks across the two options

**Lesson.** Self-identification shows curiosity, not identity. Pair numbers with research, and infer intent from behavior.

AWS · Global navVariant won

### Transcreation, not translation

A word-for-word Spanish “Start free trial” read as a command. With localization and regional teams, I rewrote it as an invitation, then audited CTAs in every supported language.

**+2%**trial CTA clicks and account creations

**Lesson.** CTA tone is part of the offer. Localization is a conversion lever, not just translation.

AWS · Industry pagesVariant won

### When “Contact sales” is the right path

Trial CTAs on industry pages drew few clicks; the audience skewed toward decision-makers. “Contact sales” CTAs routed to industry-specific sales forms.

**+4.3%**more sales contacts

**Lesson.** The opposite of Elastic’s result. The audience decides, so test the path for each one.

AWS · Sales CTAsMixed result

### “Contact us” vs. “Contact sales”

I expected the softer “Contact us” to lift clicks and form fills. It lifted clicks to the sales form, but fewer visitors completed it.

**↓ fills**more clicks, fewer completed sales forms

**Lesson.** An honest label qualifies the click. Judge sales CTAs on completed forms, not clicks.

Elastic · FrameworkFramework

### Segments and next best actions

Defined segments from first-party signals (first-time visitor, returning prospect, trialer, post-trial, customer) with one next best action and a CTA matrix by page type.

**7**lifecycle segments, each with one next step

**Lesson.** Personalization scales when segments map to funnel stages. One-off rules don’t scale.

Operating model

## How I run a program

The same seven steps in every program I’ve built, with launch emails, a public Slack channel, a shared test calendar and leadership readouts so everyone sees the results.

### Set goals and tracking

Tie each test to signups, leads or revenue, and confirm it’s measured before launch.

### Scope sample and timing

Estimate visitors and duration up front, so data is neither under- nor oversampled.

### Identify opportunities

Analytics, heatmaps, session recordings and user research.

### Write the hypothesis

If we change X, we’ll see Y, because Z. Evidence, not taste.

### Build and QA

In whichever platform fits, across devices and browsers, with no launches mid-test.

### Analyze the impact

Significance on primary and secondary KPIs, then lifetime value to size the annual gain.

### Ship, iterate, share

Winners become the default. Losers stop at the planned sample. Every result goes on the wiki.

AWS, then Elastic

## Building the program, not just the tests

A testing program scales only when marketers bring the ideas. These are the habits and tools I built at AWS and brought to Elastic.

### Deciding what to test

Many kinds of data surface opportunities, and each finding gets the right next step:

JUST DO IT  
An obvious fix, no test needed

INSTRUMENT  
A tracking or reporting gap to close first

HYPOTHESIZE  
Not working, but the fix isn’t obvious yet

INVESTIGATE  
Cause unknown, dig deeper before a hypothesis

TEST  
A clear opportunity to test and optimize

RICE = Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort

Each is scored 1–5 on defined scales. Ideas are ranked into a roadmap.

### A culture of experimentation

- **Brainstorm workshops.** Testing basics, a data deep dive, writing a strong hypothesis, then an idea brainstorm. Partners learn testing, and the team leaves with a roadmap. Run at AWS (Homepage) and Elastic (Security journey mapping).
- **Monthly program review.** Open to all marketers: running tests, last month’s results and what’s launching next. It opens with a “Which test won?” icebreaker vote, so people participate instead of multitasking.
- **Training partners.** Intro to A/B Testing: sample size, significance and MDE, so stakeholders bring stronger ideas.
- **Templates and a wiki.** The end-to-end process, scoping rules and outcomes, self-serve.
- **Keeping everyone informed.** Launch and results emails, Slack updates, a shared test calendar and leadership reviews.

| Template             | What it standardizes                                                                                                                              |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Test plan            | Background, hypothesis, estimated business impact, primary and secondary KPIs, sample size, audience, devices, and screenshots of each user flow. |
| Tracking conventions | Click names like “Element Description \[Test#\]” and a unique UTM per CTA and experience, so every click ties to signups and CRM records.         |
| Results readout      | Date range and data caveats, results with significance, a full funnel per experience, and numbered recommendations.                               |
| Process checklist    | Pre-launch, post-launch and test-end tasks, from QA and heatmaps to launch emails, a CRM check and the archive.                                   |

## Every loss changed how I test

| Test                           | What happened                                                                                                                                                                                   | Lesson                                                                                                                                                             |
|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Search guidance · Ancestry     | A banner above search results asking for more details about an ancestor, then a sticky panel. Signups fell 10% and then 2.6%, and recordings showed fewer than 20% of visitors used either one. | Use recordings to learn why. Visitors likely didn’t know more than a name and a few dates. Test 1 still raised subscription quality, so look past the signup rate. |
| Marketplace CTAs · Elastic     | Partners wouldn’t share who signed up, so the full funnel was invisible.                                                                                                                        | Make the business case explicit. Marketplace subscriptions were worth more, so even 1 signup per 100 visitors justified rolling it out.                            |
| Minor headline edits · Elastic | No difference between near-identical variants.                                                                                                                                                  | Test big swings and skip tiny copy tweaks.                                                                                                                         |
| “First Step” badge · Ancestry  | Moving or removing it cut signups 4–5% with no DNA lift.                                                                                                                                        | Some elements earn their place.
