Found by people, and by the AI that answers them

More and more questions are answered by AI overviews and chat assistants like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini before anyone clicks. My approach to search makes content easy for people, search engines and AI models to find, understand and cite.

Four ways to be found

SEO
Search engine optimization: ranking in traditional search results.
AEO
Answer engine optimization: being the direct answer in featured snippets, answer boxes and voice assistants.
GEO
Generative engine optimization: being cited in AI-generated search results, like Google’s AI Overviews and Perplexity.
LLMO
Large language model optimization: being accurately described, recommended and cited in the chat assistants people ask every day, like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini.

They overlap more than they differ. All four reward clear structure, accurate metadata and content that answers real questions.

My approach

Organize content around topics, not just pages

A topic-based structure helps people browse and helps AI models understand how ideas relate. Concept content should link clearly to the products that put those concepts to work.

Make every page machine-readable

Keywords in the headline, accurate titles and descriptions, and structured data that describes what a page is. Intros answer the question first, and FAQ sections use plain, declarative answers a model can quote correctly.

Name everything you want to be known for

AI assistants describe a company by the topics its pages cover. When Elastic’s homepage leaned on search, AI tools described Elastic mainly as a search company, so the redesign gave search, observability and security equal weight. Every statistic carries a source, because unsourced claims are less likely to be trusted or cited.

Build search into the content pipeline

Search review happens on the draft, not after launch. I built an SEO review tool so every content author gets recommendations, metadata and schema before publishing.

Keep the story consistent

AI assistants describe a company the way its content describes itself. Consistent positioning across pages leads to more accurate answers.

Start redesigns with search research

Before the Elastic homepage redesign, the SEO team scored five key pages against nine leading B2B software competitors and turned the gaps into a component checklist, a shared page structure and an internal linking map, all before a single wireframe.

Plan for every launch, not just the redesign

A page update protocol splits each page into zones with owners and update schedules, and a decision guide says which zones each kind of launch can touch. Pages stay current without rewriting the parts that rank.

Weigh search and conversion together

At Ancestry, a shorter headline on a high-ranking page lifted signups 5.6% and lifetime revenue per visitor 8.4%. We didn’t ship it, because the original headline held the keywords that brought the traffic. Instead we built a version of the page for paid traffic, where we could keep testing without risking search rank.

Measure visibility in AI answers too

Rankings and organic traffic still matter, but I also track how often our content appears in AI answers and overviews.

What we’re working on at Elastic

  • Information architecture for discoverability: a topic-based structure for concept content under /resources/learn/, with schema that cross-links concept and product pages.
  • An audit of the homepage, platform and solutions pages against nine competitors, which shaped the 2026 redesign.
  • A page update protocol so product launches update the right zones without eroding search performance.
  • An SEO review step in the content pipeline, run by authors on their own drafts.
  • Structured content and metadata across elastic.co to improve machine readability and crawlability.
  • Narrative positioning so AI answers describe Elastic accurately.

Since the content pipeline changed, Elastic’s content has been appearing more often in AI answers and overviews.
