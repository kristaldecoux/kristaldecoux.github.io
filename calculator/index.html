How the math works
  • Impact: daily visitors and conversions are totals divided by days in range; weekly is daily times 7. The new rate is the baseline times (1 + expected lift). Incremental conversions are daily visitors times the change in rate, times 365 for a year. Each funnel step carries those extra conversions forward at its current step-to-step rate.
  • Sample size: the two-proportion formula used by the Adobe Target calculator, with variance taken at both the baseline and the expected rate. Lifts are relative: a 10% lift on a 3% baseline is 3.3%.
  • Multiple variants: with the Bonferroni correction on, the significance level is divided by the number of variants compared to control, so 95% confidence with three variants tests each at 98.33%.
  • Duration: visitors per experience times the number of experiences, divided by daily visitors, rounded up. Run at least one full week, ideally two, even if the sample fills sooner.
  • Detectable lift by week: the same formula solved for the lift, given the visitors each experience would get by that week.
  • Results: a pooled two-proportion z-test for the p-value, an unpooled interval for the difference, and a delta-method interval for the relative lift. Chance to beat control is a normal approximation, not a full Bayesian model.
  • Sample ratio check: a chi-square test of visitors against the planned split. A p-value under 0.001 usually means a targeting, redirect or tracking problem to fix before trusting the results.
